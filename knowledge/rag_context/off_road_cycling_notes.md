# Off-Road Cycling Research Notes

## Physiological Demands of Simulated Off-Road Cycling Competition (PMC4657423)

### Key Findings
- Off-road cycling is characterized by high oxygen costs due to the high muscle mass simultaneously working
- Mean oxygen uptake during competition (VO2COMP) was 57.0 ± 6.8 ml·kg-1·min-1 for competitive cyclists
- High-performance athletes achieved even higher values (71.1 ml·kg-1·min-1)
- Power output during competition (POCOMP) was 2.66±0.43 W·kg-1 for competitive cyclists and 3.52 W·kg-1 for high-performance athletes
- VO2peak from laboratory tests accounted for 65% of variance in competition performance
- High aerobic capacity is a prerequisite for successful off-road cycling competition

### Physiological Measurements
- Heart rate and blood lactate concentration measures alone are not sensitive enough to assess energy requirements
- Respiratory gas and power output measures provide more accurate information about physiological demands
- Power measures from pedals represent only lower limb exertion, not overall physical activity
- Off-road cycling on rough terrain requires additional muscle mass to:
  - Handle the bike
  - Stabilize the body against gravity
  - Respond to heavy vibration

### Implications for Training
- An excellent cycling-specific capacity is a prerequisite for successful off-road cycling
- Laboratory tests can be utilized to describe semi-specific abilities of mountain bike athletes
- Data from actual competition can be useful when designing mountain bike specific training
- Training should focus on developing high aerobic capacity
- Load profiles differ during uphill, flat, and downhill cycling segments

### Relevance to Gravel Riding
- Like mountain biking, gravel riding involves varied terrain and requires handling skills
- High aerobic capacity requirements would be similar for endurance gravel events
- The need to stabilize against vibration and rough terrain is applicable to gravel riding
- Power output requirements would be comparable, especially on unpaved surfaces
- Training approaches should consider the specific physiological demands identified
