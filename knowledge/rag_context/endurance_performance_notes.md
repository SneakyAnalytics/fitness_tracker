# Endurance Performance Research Notes

## Endurance exercise performance: the physiology of champions (PMC2375555)

### Key Physiological Determinants of Endurance Performance
- Three main factors determine endurance performance:
  1. Maximal oxygen consumption (VO2 max)
  2. Lactate threshold
  3. Exercise efficiency/economy

### VO2 Max
- Champion endurance athletes have VO2 max values between 70-85 ml/kg/min
- Women typically average about 10% lower due to lower hemoglobin and higher body fat
- Values in elite athletes are 50-100% greater than normally active healthy individuals
- Key adaptations contributing to high VO2 max:
  - Increased cardiac stroke volume (most dominant factor)
  - Increased blood volume
  - Increased capillary density
  - Increased mitochondrial density in trained muscles

### Performance Velocity
- Performance velocity is determined by:
  - Level of aerobic metabolism maintained during a race
  - Upper limit is maximal oxygen uptake
  - Achieved during large muscle mass exercise
  - Represents integrative ability of multiple systems:
    - Heart's ability to generate high cardiac output
    - Total body hemoglobin
    - High muscle blood flow
    - Muscle oxygen extraction
    - Lung's ability to oxygenate blood

### Historical Context
- A.V. Hill's 1925 analysis related running speed decline with race distance to muscle fatigue
- Identified different types of fatigue:
  1. Short violent efforts
  2. Exhaustion from moderate intensity sustained over long time
  3. General "wear-and-tear" fatigue
- Early recognition of glycogen depletion in endurance events
- Scandinavian physiologists in 1930s established concepts of carbohydrate loading

### Implications for Training
- Training must address all three key physiological determinants
- High VO2 max is necessary but not sufficient for elite performance
- Lactate threshold and efficiency are equally important factors
- Individual response to training varies based on genetics, age, gender, fueling, and training history
