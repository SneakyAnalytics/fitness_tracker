## Core Training Principles

### Physiological Adaptations to Focus On

**1. Aerobic Capacity (VO₂max)**
- Foundation for all endurance performance
- Target: 57-65+ ml·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹ for competitive performance
- Developed through both low-intensity volume and high-intensity intervals

**2. Lactate Threshold**
- Determines sustainable power for long events
- Train to push threshold to 80-90% of VO₂max
- Recent research shows moderate-intensity intervals (88-93% FTP) particularly effective

**3. Sustainable Power Output**
- Target: 2.5-3.5 W/kg for competitive gravel racing
- Developed through sweet spot training and threshold work

**4. Muscular Endurance**
- Ability to maintain power over 3-8+ hours
- Built through progressive long rides and specific interval work

### Recent Research Insights (2023-2025)

**Block Training Effectiveness**
- 1-week concentrated training blocks followed by 6-day recovery highly effective
- Moderate-intensity blocks: Better for threshold improvements (4.5% gains)
- High-intensity blocks: Better for VO₂max and sprint power (2.8% gains)

**Training Intensity Distribution**
- Pyramidal model now shown equally effective as polarized approach
- Most time in low intensity, less in moderate, least in high
- Both approaches work - choose based on your schedule and preferences

**Foundation First**
- Athletes with strong low-intensity base respond better to high-intensity work
- Build aerobic foundation before emphasizing HIIT

**Strength Training Importance**
- Heavy strength training improves cycling performance
- Muscle mass linked to cognitive function during ultra-endurance events
- 2-3 sessions weekly in base phase, 1-2 in build phase

## Training Intensity Distribution

### Recommended Weekly Distribution

**Option 1: Polarized Model**
- 75-80% Low intensity (Zone 1-2)
- 5-10% Moderate intensity (Zone 3)
- 15-20% High intensity (Zone 4-5)

**Option 2: Pyramidal Model**
- 70-75% Low intensity (Zone 1-2)
- 15-20% Moderate intensity (Zone 3)
- 10-15% High intensity (Zone 4-5)

### Power Zones (% of FTP)

| Zone | % FTP | RPE | Purpose |
|------|-------|-----|---------|
| Zone 1 (Active Recovery) | <55% | 1-3 | Recovery rides |
| Zone 2 (Endurance) | 56-75% | 3-5 | Aerobic base building |
| Zone 3 (Tempo) | 76-87% | 5-7 | Muscular endurance |
| Zone 4 (Threshold) | 88-105% | 7-9 | Lactate threshold development |
| Zone 5 (VO₂max) | 106-120% | 9-10 | Aerobic capacity |
| Zone 6 (Anaerobic) | >120% | 10 | Sprint power |

## Key Workout Types

### Sweet Spot Training (88-93% FTP)
**Purpose:** Optimal balance of training stimulus and fatigue
**Structure:**
- Warmup: 10-15 min easy
- Main: 2x20 min @ 88-93% FTP (5-10 min recovery between)
- Cooldown: 10 min easy
**Progression:** Start with 2x15 min, build to 3x20 min

### Threshold Intervals (95-105% FTP)
**Purpose:** Improve lactate threshold power
**Structure:**
- Warmup: 15 min progressive
- Main: 2x20 min @ 95-100% FTP (5 min recovery)
- Cooldown: 10 min easy
**Variations:** 3x10 min, 4x8 min, or over-under intervals

### VO₂max Intervals (110-120% FTP)
**Purpose:** Enhance aerobic capacity
**Structure:**
- Warmup: 15 min with some openers
- Main: 5x4 min @ 110-120% FTP (4 min recovery)
- Cooldown: 10 min easy
**Variations:** 8x3 min, 6x5 min depending on phase

### Over-Under Intervals
**Purpose:** Improve ability to clear lactate
**Structure:**
- Warmup: 15 min
- Main: 3 sets of (5 min @ 95% FTP + 1 min @ 110% FTP), 5 min recovery between sets
- Cooldown: 10 min

### Sprint Intervals
**Purpose:** Develop anaerobic capacity and sprint power
**Structure:**
- Warmup: 20 min with progressive efforts
- Main: 8x30 sec all-out (4-5 min recovery between)
- Cooldown: 10 min easy

### Long Endurance Rides
**Purpose:** Build aerobic base and fat oxidation
**Structure:**
- 3-6 hours @ Zone 2 (65-75% FTP)
- Include terrain-specific elements (gravel, hills)
- Practice nutrition and pacing strategies

*Focus: Aerobic development, strength foundation*

| Day | Workout | Duration | Intensity | Notes |
|-----|---------|----------|-----------|-------|
| Monday | Rest/Recovery | 0-30 min | - | Light mobility or complete rest |
| Tuesday | Strength + Endurance | 1.5-2 hrs | Low-Mod | Strength training + Zone 2 ride |
| Wednesday | Aerobic Development | 2-3 hrs | Low | Zone 2 ride with varied terrain |
| Thursday | Strength + Sweet Spot | 1.5-2 hrs | Moderate | Strength + 2x15-20 min sweet spot |
| Friday | Active Recovery | 1 hr | Very Low | Zone 1 easy spin |
| Saturday | Long Endurance | 3-5 hrs | Low | Zone 2 with some Zone 3 efforts |
| Sunday | Skills + Endurance | 1.5-2 hrs | Low-Mod | Technical practice with efforts |

| Wednesday | Endurance | 2-3 hrs | Low | Zone 2 with terrain variation |

| Saturday | Race Simulation | 3-5 hrs | Mixed | Long ride with race-intensity bursts |
| Sunday | Strength + Endurance | 2-2.5 hrs | Low-Mod | Maintenance strength + Zone 2 |

*Focus: Race-specific preparation, maintaining fitness*

| Day | Workout | Duration | Intensity | Notes |
|-----|---------|----------|-----------|-------|

## Periodization Strategy

### Annual Planning Framework

**1. Off-Season (8-12 weeks)**
- Build strength foundation
- Address weaknesses and imbalances
- Include cross-training (running, skiing, etc.)
- Maintain aerobic fitness with easy rides

**2. Base Period (12-16 weeks)**
- Progressive aerobic development
- Sweet spot and tempo work
- Continued strength training
- Gradually increase volume

**3. Build Period (8-12 weeks)**
- Event-specific intervals
- Threshold and VO₂max work
- Strength maintenance
- Race-specific terrain practice

**4. Peak/Race Period (Variable)**
- Maintain fitness with reduced volume
- Sharpen with specific intervals
- Practice race execution
- Strategic tapering

**5. Recovery Period (2-4 weeks)**
- Active recovery between blocks
- Mental refreshment
- Evaluate and plan next cycle

### Block Training Application

**When to Use Training Blocks:**
- Stuck at performance plateau
- Need rapid improvement in specific area
- 6-8 weeks before target event

**MIT Block (Moderate-Intensity):**
- 6 sessions over 7 days
- 5-7 x 10-14 min @ 88-93% FTP
- Focus on threshold power improvement
- Follow with 6 days active recovery

**HIT Block (High-Intensity):**
- 5 sessions over 6 days  
- Multiple short intervals @ 110-120% FTP
- Focus on VO₂max and sprint power
- Follow with 6 days active recovery

## Strength Training

### Base Phase (2-3x/week)
**Focus:** Building foundation, correcting imbalances

**Key Exercises:**
- **Lower Body:** Squats, deadlifts, lunges (3x12-15 reps)
- **Core:** Planks, side planks, bird dogs (3x30-60 sec)
- **Upper Body:** Push-ups, rows, pull-ups (3x10-15 reps)
- **Single-Leg:** Bulgarian split squats, single-leg deadlifts (3x10 each)

### Build Phase (1-2x/week)
**Focus:** Power development, cycling-specific strength

**Key Exercises:**
- **Power:** Box jumps, jump squats (3x6-8 reps)
- **Heavy Compound:** Squats, deadlifts (3x6-8 reps, heavier weight)
- **Core Anti-Rotation:** Pallof press, Russian twists (3x12-15)
- **Stability:** Single-leg exercises with balance challenge

### In-Season Maintenance (1x/week)
**Focus:** Maintain strength gains, prevent injury

**Key Exercises:**
- **Compound Movements:** Moderate weight, 2x8-10 reps
- **Core Stability:** 2x30-45 sec holds
- **Unilateral Work:** 2x8-10 each leg

## Nutrition Guidelines

### Daily Nutrition Targets

**Carbohydrates:**
- Moderate training: 5-7 g/kg body weight
- High volume training: 8-10 g/kg body weight
- Example (75kg athlete): 375-750g daily depending on load

**Protein:**
- 1.6-2.0 g/kg body weight daily
- Example (75kg athlete): 120-150g daily
- Distribute across meals (20-40g per meal)

**Fats:**
- 20-35% of total daily calories
- Prioritize healthy fats (nuts, avocado, olive oil, fatty fish)

**Hydration:**
- Minimum 2-3 liters daily
- Additional 500-750ml per hour of training
- Monitor urine color (pale yellow target)

### During Training/Racing

**<90 minutes:** Water only (or small amount carbs if high intensity)

**90-150 minutes:**
- 30-60g carbs per hour
- 500-750ml fluid per hour

**>150 minutes:**
- 60-90g carbs per hour (train gut to tolerate)
- 500-750ml fluid per hour
- 500-700mg sodium per hour (hot conditions)

### Recovery Nutrition

**Immediate Post-Workout (within 30-60 min):**
- 20-25g protein
- 60-100g carbohydrate
- Example: Recovery shake or meal

**Hydration Recovery:**
- Replace 150% of fluid lost
- Weigh before/after for accuracy
- Include electrolytes

## Recovery Protocols

### Daily Recovery Practices

**Sleep:**
- 7-9 hours quality sleep nightly
- Consistent sleep/wake schedule
- Dark, cool environment

**Hydration:**
- Maintain throughout day
- Pale yellow urine color
- Increase in hot weather

**Nutrition:**
- Follow recovery nutrition guidelines
- Anti-inflammatory foods
- Adequate protein distribution

**Mobility:**
- 10-15 minutes daily
- Target tight areas
- Dynamic stretching pre-workout, static post-workout

### Weekly Recovery Strategies

**Rest Days:**
- At least 1 complete rest day weekly
- More during high-load weeks

**Active Recovery:**
- 1-2 easy sessions (30-60 min) weekly
- Zone 1 intensity only
- Helps flush metabolic waste

**Soft Tissue Work:**
- Foam rolling 10-15 min, 2-3x weekly
- Massage when available
- Target areas under load

### Monitoring Recovery

**Morning Metrics:**
- Resting heart rate (5+ bpm increase = under-recovered)
- Heart rate variability (if tracking)
- Perceived fatigue (1-10 scale)
- Sleep quality

**Performance Indicators:**
- Power output at given RPE
- Ability to complete prescribed intervals
- Motivation and enthusiasm for training

**When to Adjust:**
- Consistently elevated resting HR
- Multiple failed workouts
- Persistent fatigue or mood changes
- Illness or injury signs

## Performance Metrics

### Key Numbers to Track

**FTP (Functional Threshold Power):**
- Test every 6-8 weeks
- 20-min or ramp test protocols
- Use to set training zones

**Power Benchmarks:**
| Duration | Target (W/kg) | Purpose |
|----------|---------------|---------|
| 30 sec | 6-8+ | Sprint power |
| 1 min | 5-6+ | VO₂max capacity |
| 5 min | 4.5-5.5+ | VO₂max endurance |
| 20 min | 3.5-4.5+ | Threshold power |
| 60 min | 3.0-4.0+ | Sustainable race pace |

**Training Load Metrics:**
- Weekly TSS (Training Stress Score)
- CTL (Chronic Training Load / fitness)
- ATL (Acute Training Load / fatigue)
- TSB (Training Stress Balance / form)

**Body Metrics:**
- Weight (stable or trending appropriately)
- Body composition (maintain muscle mass)
- Resting heart rate

### Setting Goals

**Process Goals:**
- Complete prescribed workouts
- Maintain consistency (90%+ completion rate)
- Practice race nutrition on long rides

**Performance Goals:**
- Specific FTP targets
- Power duration benchmarks
- Event time goals

**Outcome Goals:**
- Event placements
- Personal bests
- Qualification achievements

## Adapting to Your Individual Needs

### Time-Constrained Training (5-8 hours/week)

**Priorities:**
- 2-3 high-quality interval sessions
- 1 long endurance ride (weekend)
- Strength training 1-2x/week
- Emphasize intensity over volume

### Moderate Time Availability (8-12 hours/week)

**Priorities:**
- Balance intensity and volume
- 3-4 key workouts weekly
- Progressive long ride development
- Full strength program

### High Time Availability (12+ hours/week)

**Priorities:**
- Emphasize volume for aerobic development
- Multiple long rides weekly
- Full complement of intervals
- Comprehensive strength and skills work

## Final Recommendations

### Keys to Success

1. **Consistency over perfection** - Complete 85-90% of planned training
2. **Progressive overload** - Gradually increase stress over time
3. **Adequate recovery** - Adaptations happen during rest
4. **Specific preparation** - Train the demands of your events
5. **Enjoy the process** - Sustainable training = long-term success

### Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Too much intensity** - Most athletes train moderate intensity too hard
2. **Insufficient recovery** - More is not always better
3. **Neglecting strength** - Full-body conditioning improves cycling performance
4. **Poor nutrition** - Fuel quality and timing matter
5. **Ignoring warning signs** - Address fatigue, illness, injury early

### When to Seek Professional Help

- Persistent performance plateaus
- Recurring injuries or illness
- Questions about training progression
- Event-specific preparation needs
- Optimization for specific goals
